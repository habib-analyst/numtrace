A number can be real and still be wrong. I built a small clean-room
experiment (scripted agents, fully synthetic data — not a reproduction of
any paper's results) that splits a numerical claim into value, binding and
support: a scope-blind first-hit agent quoted real filing numbers but got
the group scope wrong on every group-total question, while filtering to
the group's members, summing, and checking the binding fixed it without
breaking correct single-firm answers. The lesson travels: when an agent
quotes a number, ask not just "is this number somewhere in the source?"
but "does it belong to the entity, period and scope claimed?"
